GAME 01 · CALL OF DUTY: MOBILE

From beginner to smarter player

Call of Duty: Mobile combines fast Multiplayer matches with larger Battle Royale experiences. The official game page describes Multiplayer as classic team-based combat with multiple maps and modes, while Battle Royale gives players a much larger space to manage. Understanding that difference is the first step toward improving.

For a beginner, do not try to master everything in one day. Pick one mode. Learn the objective. Learn the map. Learn where your teammates normally move. Then pay attention to what happens immediately before you make a mistake. That makes practice much more useful than simply playing more matches.

In objective modes, remember that the objective is the reason the match exists. A player can have a good individual moment and still make a poor team decision. GameVerse encourages readers to ask: Where is the objective? Where is my team? What area needs support? What route gives me another option if the first plan stops working?

In Battle Royale, the thinking becomes broader. Position, movement, awareness and planning matter because the area is larger and the situation changes. Good decisions are often made before the pressure arrives.

Beginner Skills

Learn controls, one mode, one map and the objective. Do not overload yourself with advanced ideas before the basics become comfortable.

Map Awareness

Study common routes, important areas and places where a player can become predictable. Change patterns when opponents start reading you.

Team Awareness

Watch where teammates are and what area is under pressure. Your next decision should make sense in the context of the whole match.

What actually creates improvement?

  • Choose one skill per practice session.
  • Review repeated mistakes instead of blaming one unlucky moment.
  • Do not change your whole play style after one bad game.
  • Use the map and objective as sources of information.
  • Stay calm enough to notice what caused a mistake.
GAME 02 · FREE FIRE

Movement, awareness and character choice

Free Fire is designed around quick matches and changing situations, but that speed does not mean every decision should be rushed. The official Free Fire site also maintains a dedicated character catalogue, which shows that character identity and collection are important parts of the game experience.

The first thing a new player should practise is awareness. Before moving, look at the space around you and think about what information you have. If you move into a new area, ask what happens if the situation changes. A good position gives you choices; a bad position removes them.

Character choice should be treated as part of strategy. A character can be visually impressive, but understanding its role or ability is more useful than choosing only by appearance. Build your choice around how you like to play and then practise that style until your decisions become more consistent.

Teamwork matters too. Share useful information, watch your teammates' direction and be willing to change the plan when the match gives you new information. A flexible team usually adapts better than a team that refuses to change its first idea.

Awareness

Before committing to a move, understand what you can see, where you can go next and what information is missing.

Position

Prefer areas that give you useful information and more than one way to react if the situation changes.

Characters

Read about a character's identity and ability, then decide whether that role fits the way you naturally play.

Free Fire practice routine

  • Session one: focus only on movement.
  • Session two: focus on awareness and positioning.
  • Session three: study one character choice.
  • Session four: combine the skills and review which decisions improved.
GAME 03 · DREAM LEAGUE SOCCER

Build the team and understand the football

First Touch Games describes Dream League Soccer as a football game where players can build a dream team from more than 4,000 FIFPRO licensed players and progress through divisions. That makes squad construction a major part of the experience.

A common beginner mistake is to focus only on the most exciting attackers. Football works because different jobs fit together. A balanced squad needs players who can defend, progress the ball, create chances and finish them. Once the squad has a clear balance, the tactics become easier to understand.

During matches, do not force every attack. Sometimes the best move is a safe pass that changes the shape of the defence. Look at space before choosing the pass. Keep enough structure behind the ball so a mistake does not immediately become a dangerous counter.

When you lose a match, identify the first decision that created the problem. Was the pass too risky? Did a defender move too far? Did the attack become predictable? That answer gives you something specific to practise.

Squad Balance

Think about every line of the team rather than collecting famous names without considering how the players work together.

Passing

Use possession to create better choices. The best pass is often the one that improves the next action.

Defensive Shape

Do not move the whole team forward at once. Keep enough structure to respond when possession changes.

Football mindset

Do not judge your entire team by one match. Look for patterns over several games, then change one thing at a time. A clear process produces more useful improvement than constant rebuilding.

GAME 04 · eFOOTBALL

Understand special players, tactics and tempo

Konami's official eFootball site explains that the game includes Authentic Team and Dream Team experiences, while special-player lists can contain Epic and Highlight versions. The current official information says Epic players reflect standout performances from a specific season and can have unique card designs based on photos from that season.

That makes player collection more interesting because the same footballer can be represented through different versions with different presentation and attributes. GameVerse now explains the idea so visitors understand what a special card is instead of simply seeing a picture with no context.

On the pitch, tempo is the key idea. You do not have to attack at the same speed throughout a match. Slow possession can move defenders and create space. Quick movement can exploit that space. Defending also requires patience because pulling players out of position too early can create openings.

Use formations that you understand. When something goes wrong, decide whether the problem came from the formation, the player role or the decision you made in the moment. Make one adjustment and test it before rebuilding the whole squad.

Formations

Choose a system you can explain. Knowing where players should be is more important than constantly changing shapes.

Tempo

Change speed deliberately. Possession and acceleration are both useful when they are used for a reason.

Special Players

Learn what an Epic or Highlight version means before judging its value. Presentation and attributes are part of the difference.

How to review a difficult match

  • Find the first repeated mistake.
  • Check whether the problem was tactical or simply poor execution.
  • Change one thing, test it, then decide what to do next.
