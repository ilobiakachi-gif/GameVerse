GAME 01 · ULTIMATE GUIDE

Call of Duty: Mobile

Call of Duty: Mobile gives players fast team matches as well as larger Battle Royale experiences. There is no single strategy that works perfectly everywhere. In Multiplayer, understanding the objective, the routes around a map and the position of your teammates can matter just as much as individual aim. In Battle Royale, planning movement, paying attention to the area around you and deciding when to engage become important parts of surviving longer.

A strong beginner goal is consistency. Instead of changing every setting and tactic after one bad match, choose one skill to improve. Learn a map, practise controlling movement, become familiar with a favourite mode and review what went wrong. Small improvements repeated over many matches are usually more useful than searching for one magical trick.

Understanding Multiplayer

Different modes reward different decisions. If a mode has an objective, helping with that objective can be more valuable than running around looking for every possible fight. Watch where your teammates are moving and learn common paths so you are not surprised by the same situation repeatedly.

  • Know what the match requires to win.
  • Use cover and avoid predictable movement.
  • Change routes when opponents expect you.

Battle Royale Mindset

Battle Royale rewards awareness and planning. Moving constantly without a reason can leave you in a poor position, while waiting too long can also limit your options. Before moving, think about where you want to end up and what you will do if the situation changes.

  • Move with a purpose.
  • Notice changes around the map.
  • Think about your next position early.

How to Improve

After a difficult match, ask what decision could improve next time. Maybe you rushed without information, stayed in one place too long or ignored the objective. Pick one weakness and practise it for several matches before moving to another skill.

  • Practise one mode consistently.
  • Learn from repeated mistakes.
  • Focus on progress, not one perfect match.

Extra tips and tricks

  • Do not rush automatically. Slow down when you lack information.
  • Use teammates as information. Their positions can reveal where pressure is building.
  • Break predictable habits. Change your route when the same one keeps failing.
  • Learn one thing per session. Focused practice is powerful.
  • Stay calm after mistakes. One bad decision does not need to create several more.
GAME 02 · ULTIMATE GUIDE

Free Fire

Free Fire is a fast-paced game where survival, awareness and adaptation can be just as important as direct combat. Every match develops differently, which means copying the exact same plan every time is not always the best approach. The beginning of a match is about getting organised and understanding your surroundings. As the match continues, positioning and timing become increasingly important because the safest route and strongest position can change.

Beginners often improve faster when they separate learning from winning. Spend some matches concentrating on movement, map awareness or decision-making rather than expecting every game to be perfect. Once the controls feel natural, it becomes easier to notice the bigger picture and make calmer choices.

Start of the Match

The early stage is a chance to become comfortable with the match and prepare for what comes next. Avoid random decisions simply because the action is fast.

  • Learn familiar areas gradually.
  • Stay aware instead of rushing blindly.
  • Prepare an option for your next movement.

Positioning and Movement

A good position gives you options. Think about what information you can gather and where you can go if the situation becomes difficult.

  • Keep more than one possible route.
  • Avoid standing exposed without a reason.
  • Do not move simply because everyone else is moving.

Team Play

When playing with teammates, awareness and simple communication reduce confusion. Sharing useful information can help everyone make better decisions.

  • Pay attention to the team's direction.
  • Share useful information clearly.
  • Adapt instead of insisting on one plan.

Extra tips and tricks

  • Before moving, ask where you will go if the first route becomes unsafe.
  • Do not panic when the match changes quickly; use the information available.
  • Practise movement without judging every match only by whether you win.
  • Patience can be useful: waiting for better information is sometimes smarter.
  • Review the moment before a mistake to understand the decision.
GAME 03 · ULTIMATE GUIDE

Dream League Soccer

Dream League Soccer combines squad building with decisions on the pitch. Building a successful team is not only about collecting strong individual players. Balance matters because a team needs players who can support different phases of play. Your formation should fit the style you understand. Constantly changing formations after every difficult match can make it harder to learn how your players move.

During matches, simple football ideas can make a major difference: keep the ball when the risky option is unnecessary, look for space before forcing an attack and maintain enough defensive structure to react when possession is lost.

Build a Balanced Squad

Think about roles rather than only overall ratings. A balanced squad gives you options when a match becomes difficult.

  • Consider different positions and roles.
  • Do not neglect defensive balance.
  • Develop a team that fits your approach.

Smarter Attacking

Every possession does not need to become an immediate forward attack. Use passing to create better options and recycle possession when needed.

  • Look for space before forcing passes.
  • Move the ball with purpose.
  • Be patient when the attack is crowded.

Better Defending

Defending starts before an opponent reaches your goal. Good positioning and maintaining shape reduce the need for desperate recovery.

  • Watch the space around defenders.
  • Do not pull everyone out of position.
  • Stay patient and react to play.

Extra tips and tricks

  • Learn one formation properly before constantly switching.
  • A safe pass can be better than forcing a difficult one.
  • Notice your team shape before committing too many players forward.
  • Vary your attacks if an opponent reads your favourite move.
  • Use losses to identify whether passing, positioning or defending caused the problem.
GAME 04 · ULTIMATE GUIDE

eFootball

eFootball rewards patience, tactical awareness and understanding how your players fit together. Whether you are exploring Authentic Team or building a Dream Team, the aim is not simply to collect names. A squad works best when you understand how its players, formation and style connect. Learning a small number of tactical ideas can be more valuable than changing the entire team after one difficult match.

On the pitch, controlling the tempo can be important. Not every possession needs a fast attack. Sometimes the best decision is to keep the ball, wait for movement and choose a higher-quality option. Defending also requires patience because moving a player out of position too early can create space.

Building Your Team

Build with a plan. Consider how players complement each other and whether the team suits the style you want to use.

  • Choose a clear style.
  • Understand player roles.
  • Make changes for a reason.

Controlling the Match

Good football is about recognising when to speed up and when to slow down. Keep possession when necessary and look for movement.

  • Do not rush every attack.
  • Watch off-ball movement.
  • Change tempo when appropriate.

Defensive Discipline

Patience is a defensive skill. Pulling players away from useful positions too early can create problems.

  • Protect important spaces.
  • Avoid unnecessary chasing.
  • Recover your shape after losing the ball.

Extra tips and tricks

  • Do not make a major tactical change after every loss.
  • Use the simplest effective option instead of forcing highlights.
  • Watch opponent habits and adapt.
  • Patience and positioning can be more reliable than constant aggressive movement.
  • Improve one part of your game at a time.
