Game 01 · Detailed Guide

Call of Duty: Mobile

Call of Duty: Mobile gives players different ways to compete. Multiplayer focuses on team-based modes and objectives, while Battle Royale places players on larger maps where awareness, movement and decision-making become important. The best approach is not simply to rush every situation: first understand the mode, then pay attention to your surroundings and work with your team when possible.

For a new player, improving gradually is usually more useful than changing everything at once. Spend time learning a map or mode, notice where teammates are moving, and think about what information you have before deciding where to go next.

TIP 1 · LEARN THE OBJECTIVE

Before a match, know what your team needs to accomplish. Playing around the objective can be more useful than chasing every opponent.

TIP 2 · CHECK THE MAP

Use the information available to you. Notice teammate positions and avoid repeatedly walking into the same dangerous area without a plan.

TIP 3 · CHANGE YOUR PLAN

If one route is not working, try a different path, support another teammate or focus on a different part of the objective.

TIP 4 · REVIEW YOUR MATCH

After a difficult round, ask what caused the problem: poor positioning, rushing too early or ignoring the objective. Fix one habit at a time.

Multiplayer

Multiplayer includes team modes with different objectives. A useful beginner habit is to play with the mode in mind instead of treating every match exactly the same.

  • Stay aware of teammates.
  • Use cover and routes sensibly.
  • Do not repeat the same mistake immediately.

Battle Royale

Battle Royale uses larger spaces and changes the importance of movement and positioning. Think ahead about where you want to go and what information you need before committing to a direction.

  • Move with a purpose.
  • Watch changing conditions.
  • Choose safer positions when appropriate.

Beginner Focus

Do not try to master every weapon, map and mode in one day. Pick one or two areas to practise and build familiarity over time.

  • Practise one mode repeatedly.
  • Learn common routes.
  • Improve one weakness per session.

Quick tricks to remember

  • Slow down when you are unsure of what is ahead; awareness can be more valuable than rushing.
  • If your team is grouped elsewhere, consider whether going alone actually helps the objective.
  • When a tactic stops working, changing your position can be smarter than forcing the same approach again.
  • Use practice and lower-pressure matches to learn unfamiliar modes before expecting perfect results.
Game 02 · Detailed Guide

Free Fire

Free Fire is built around fast matches where survival, positioning and adapting to changing situations matter. The game includes Battle Royale, Clash Squad and other modes, so your priorities can change depending on what you are playing. In a survival-style match, the goal is not to be everywhere at once. Good decisions about where to move and when to stay patient can make a major difference.

A useful way to improve is to develop a routine: get comfortable with the controls, learn to read the area around you and avoid making every decision in a hurry. As each match develops, stay flexible rather than following one plan no matter what happens.

TIP 1 · START WITH A PLAN

Choose an early direction and get familiar with it instead of changing your mind every few seconds.

TIP 2 · POSITION MATTERS

Think about what areas give you useful information and options for your next move rather than standing in an exposed place.

TIP 3 · DO NOT PANIC

When the situation changes, take a moment to decide what information you have and choose the next safe option.

TIP 4 · ADAPT EACH MATCH

No two matches develop exactly the same way. Treat your plan as flexible and adjust when conditions change.

Early Match

The beginning is a good time to get oriented and prepare for the next stage. Learn the map gradually and avoid turning every early encounter into the only thing that matters.

  • Get comfortable with your controls.
  • Check your surroundings.
  • Plan your next movement.

Mid Match

As the situation changes, information becomes important. Pay attention to where action is happening and decide whether joining it improves your position.

  • Avoid unnecessary detours.
  • Keep an escape option.
  • Move before you are forced to rush.

Team Play

When playing with others, simple communication and staying aware of teammates can reduce confusion. A coordinated plan is usually easier to follow than everyone making unrelated decisions.

  • Stay aware of teammates.
  • Share useful information.
  • Adjust when the team changes direction.

Quick tricks to remember

  • Before moving, think about where you will go next if your first route becomes unsafe.
  • Practise movement and controls separately from trying to win every match.
  • Do not copy a strategy blindly; a tactic that worked in one match may not fit the next one.
  • Patience is a skill: waiting for better information can sometimes be smarter than rushing.
Game 03 · Detailed Guide

Dream League Soccer

Dream League Soccer combines building a football squad with playing matches. Your choices away from the pitch can affect how your team feels during a game, while decisions on the pitch involve passing, movement, defending and choosing when to attack. A strong team is not only about collecting highly rated players; it also helps when the squad has balance and your formation fits the way you want to play.

For beginners, it is useful to learn a simple style first. Keep possession when the safe pass is available, look for space rather than forcing every attack, and remember that defending begins with good positioning before the opponent reaches your goal.

TIP 1 · BUILD FOR BALANCE

Think about different roles in your team. A balanced squad can give you more options than focusing on only one type of player.

TIP 2 · PASS WITH A PURPOSE

Use passes to move the ball into better areas. If a risky forward pass is blocked, recycling possession can be the better choice.

TIP 3 · WATCH YOUR SHAPE

When attacking, remember what happens if you lose the ball. Keeping a sensible structure can make transitions easier.

TIP 4 · PRACTISE ONE FORMATION

Spend enough matches learning one formation before changing constantly. You will better understand where your players naturally move.

Building Your Club

Squad building is a long-term part of the game. Add players in positions that improve your options and learn how their roles fit together.

  • Check weak positions first.
  • Keep useful substitutes.
  • Improve gradually.

Attacking

Good attacks usually involve patience and movement. Look for open teammates and avoid forcing the same pass repeatedly when the opponent is ready for it.

  • Use width when useful.
  • Look for open space.
  • Mix short and forward passes.

Defending

Defending is easier when you stay organized. Pulling players out of position too early can leave useful spaces behind them.

  • Protect important spaces.
  • Stay patient when possible.
  • Recover into shape.

Quick tricks to remember

  • If an attack keeps failing, change the direction of play instead of repeating the same route.
  • Learn when to keep the ball rather than always playing the fastest possible pass.
  • After conceding, focus on the next few decisions instead of trying to fix everything in one attack.
  • Experiment with formations gradually so you can tell which change actually helped.
Game 04 · Detailed Guide

eFootball

eFootball offers different ways to play, including using ready-made Authentic Teams or building a custom Dream Team. Dream Team allows you to sign players and managers, register them in your Game Plan and develop eligible players over time. The game also includes tutorials and Smart Assist options to help players learn the basics. Your tactical choices matter because the way your players and manager fit a chosen playstyle can influence how the team performs.

On the pitch, focus on simple decisions first: keep the ball when a safe option exists, move into useful space and defend as a unit rather than dragging players away from important areas. As you improve, experiment with tactics and player development based on how you actually like to play.

TIP 1 · LEARN THE BASICS FIRST

Use tutorials and practice to understand passing, movement and defending before worrying about advanced tactics.

TIP 2 · BUILD A CLEAR PLAYSTYLE

Choose a style that fits how you want to play, then make squad and manager choices that support it.

TIP 3 · DEVELOP WITH A REASON

Player development is more useful when it supports the role you actually want that player to perform.

TIP 4 · DO NOT FORCE ATTACKS

If the forward route is closed, reset the play and search for another opening instead of giving the ball away repeatedly.

Authentic Team

This option lets you use ready-to-play clubs or national teams. It is useful for learning match basics without first building a full custom squad.

  • Learn the team's shape.
  • Practise passing patterns.
  • Understand player movement.

Dream Team

Dream Team lets you create your own squad by signing players and managers. You can register players, take part in matches and develop eligible players.

  • Build around a clear idea.
  • Check squad balance.
  • Develop players gradually.

Tactics & Matches

eFootball includes multiple team playstyles. The goal is not to copy every tactic you see, but to find one that fits your preferred decisions and squad.

  • Use space intelligently.
  • Keep a defensive structure.
  • Adjust when a plan fails.

Quick tricks to remember

  • When under pressure, a simple safe pass can be better than a difficult forward ball.
  • Watch where defenders are positioned before starting an attack through the middle.
  • Use practice matches to test tactical changes one at a time.
  • Build your Dream Team around roles and playstyle, not only the highest numbers you can find.
